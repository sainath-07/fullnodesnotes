1. What are these questions called?

Logical / Lateral Thinking Puzzles

The cake-cutting question you mentioned is commonly grouped under logic puzzles, brain teasers, lateral-thinking puzzles, mathematical puzzles, and interview puzzle questions.

Important: There is no single official name for every puzzle in this family. Interviewers often use these questions to test problem solving rather than a particular programming language or data structure.

For example, “cut a cake into 4 pieces in 2 tries” tests whether you can change the way you visualize the problem. A normal approach may think only about cuts from the top; a better approach considers cuts in three dimensions.

2. How to approach these puzzles
① Clarify the rules

Ask what counts as a cut, whether pieces can be moved, whether cuts must be straight, and whether dimensions matter.

② Draw it

Use a quick sketch. Many “impossible” puzzles become simple when represented visually.

③ Challenge assumptions

Ask yourself: “What am I assuming that the question never said?”

④ Look for a different dimension

Try time, space, ordering, grouping, probability, or an unusual interpretation.

Interview habit: Don't jump straight to the answer. Explain your assumptions and thought process. The interviewer is often evaluating how you attack an unfamiliar problem.

5. Interview Tips
Don't worry if you cannot solve it immediately. These questions are intentionally unfamiliar. Interviewers often care about how you explore the problem.
  1. Restate the problem in your own words.
  2. Ask about ambiguous rules.
  3. Start with a small example.
  4. Draw a picture or table.
  5. Write down what is fixed and what can change.
  6. Try the obvious solution first, then challenge it.
  7. Look for symmetry, grouping, parity, binary choices, or repeated states.
  8. Before optimizing, make sure the basic solution is correct.
  9. Explain failed approaches instead of silently abandoning them.
  10. When you find a trick, explain why it works.
🧠 A powerful checklist

1. What are the rules?

2. What am I assuming?

3. Can I draw it?

4. Can I split the possibilities?

5. Can I work backwards?

6. Can I change the dimension or representation?

7. Is there an invariant or repeated state?
